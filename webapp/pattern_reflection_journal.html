🜛 Kairos Reflection Journal

A pattern-reflection tool for whatever you bring it — texts, relationships, work, habits, cycles.
What this is: an interpretive self-reflection tool — the same category as tarot, the I Ching, or a structured journaling prompt deck. It is not a scientific measurement or prediction tool.

How the "reading" works: it reduces the word count and letter count of whatever text you enter to a single digit (0–9), the same digit-root arithmetic used throughout this project's Container Rule engine, and maps the result to one of ten named stages. That math will find a "pattern" in any sufficiently long text — Scripture, a grocery list, or random keyboard mashing — with equal reliability. That's a property of modular arithmetic, not evidence about the text. Treat every reading as a writing prompt, not a verdict.

Your data: everything you enter is stored only in this browser's local storage. Nothing is sent anywhere. Use "Export data" on any journal tab to back it up or move it to another device.

Not intended for medical, legal, financial, or diagnostic decisions.

What you can do here

Bring anything — a Bible or Quran passage, a paragraph about a relationship, a section of your resume, a note about what you ate today, a description of a recurring argument. The engine doesn't need to know what kind of thing it is; it just reflects the same ten-stage lens back at it, and gives you a prompt to think with.

1. Pattern Scan

Paste any text or write about any topic. Get a stage reading, the habits typically associated with it, and a reflective prompt. Save it to build a running journal.

2. Cycle Journal

Track named life areas — a relationship, a job search, a health habit — over time, and see where you keep landing.

3. Habit Log

A checklist of plain-language habits per stage. Check off what you notice in yourself, and watch which stages recur most.
