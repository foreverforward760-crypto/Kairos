🜛 Kairos Reflection Journal

A pattern-reflection tool for whatever you bring it — texts, relationships, work, habits, cycles.
What this is: an interpretive self-reflection tool — the same category as tarot, the I Ching, or a structured journaling prompt deck.

How the reading works: the ten stages form a wheel, not a ladder — 0 through 9 and back to 0. The cycle moves forward by tumbling: at the even stages (2, 4, 6, 8) the outer situation looks solid while the inner state is quietly unsettled underneath it; at the odd stages (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) the outer situation looks unsettled while the inner state is actually gaining clarity. Neither side can hold forever, so the system keeps tumbling forward — that trade-off is the engine, not a flaw in it. Every reading locates where your words currently sit on that wheel, using the shape of the text itself (word count and letter count, reduced the same way throughout this project) — point it at anything: a passage, a conversation, a decision, a single sentence.

Your data: the base reading (the stage, the habits, the quote) runs entirely on your device using simple word/letter arithmetic — nothing is sent anywhere for that part, and "Export data" on any journal tab backs it up or moves it to another device. If you tap "Ask Kairos to go deeper" on a reading, the text you're reading gets sent to Claude (Anthropic's AI) to generate a personalized narrative, a story parallel, and follow-up questions — that's the one part of this app that isn't fully local. As with anything you type into any online tool: don't paste something you wouldn't want to leave your device, and use the same judgment here you'd use anywhere else online.

Not intended for medical, legal, financial, or diagnostic decisions.

What you can do here

Bring anything — a Bible or Quran passage, a paragraph about a relationship, a section of your resume, a note about what you ate today, a description of a recurring argument. The engine doesn't need to know what kind of thing it is; it just reflects the same ten-stage lens back at it, and gives you a prompt to think with.

1. Pattern Scan

Paste any text or write about any topic. Get a stage reading, the habits typically associated with it, and a reflective prompt. Save it to build a running journal.

2. Cycle Journal

Track named life areas — a relationship, a job search, a health habit — over time, and see where you keep landing.

3. Habit Log

A checklist of plain-language habits per stage. Check off what you notice in yourself, and watch which stages recur most.

→ Read the Stage Guide first if you want to know what the ten stages mean before you start.

What's new

A few pieces of this project's own framework beyond raw digit-root math, added honestly:

  • Inversion — every reading now shows whether the stage reads as physically stable/unstable vs. consciously stable/unstable, ported from the Container Rule engine's own labeling.
  • Stage 5 fork — Stage 5 readings now name the three real directions (Advance / Freeze / Crisis) instead of just a habit list.
  • Trap accumulation — the Cycle Journal now flags when an area's check-ins repeat Stage 8, using the same consecutive-count logic as the engine's trap detector.
  • Deeper zoom — readings can now drill a third level down (nano-position), same transparent math, smaller slice, opt-in.
  • Consistency signal — the Cycle Journal now computes real entropy over your own check-in history per area, showing whether it clusters on one stage or scatters.

Left out on purpose: a formula that scores your odds of success at a decision (its weights were never fit to real outcomes — a percentage would be invented, not computed), and any claim that this can tell real signal from meaningless text. The consistency signal above measures how consistent you've been, not whether your words mean anything.

About the micro-position (e.g. "4.5"): Pattern Scan also reads a smaller slice of what you wrote — usually your closing sentence — through the same digit-root formula, giving a second digit 0–9 nested inside the stage. Across every stage, .5 always lands on a threshold/pivot flavor and .9 always lands on a transparency/release flavor; the digits in between (.1–.4, .6–.8) use one shared, generic set of labels rather than 81 individually-authored ones. It's the same math run twice, not a more precise instrument.
